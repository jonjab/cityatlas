China Urban Growth Ratlas (1985–2015)

A Geospatial Exploration of 30 Years of Physical Expansion Across 47 Chinese Metropolises

China Urban Growth Ratlas

中国城市空间扩展图集 · 1985–2015

Between 1985 and 2015, the People's Republic of China underwent the largest physical urbanization process in human history. This ratlas maps the spatial morphology, expansion rings, China Railway (12306) and municipal subway networks, and quantitative growth trajectories across 47 diverse Chinese cities ranked by population, with global benchmarks in Chicago, Milwaukee, and Charlotte, accompanied by historical map plates from the American Geographical Society Library (AGSL).

NoteScope & sample selection: Excluding Tier-1 superstar megacities

The 47 cities in this atlas were deliberately selected to exclude China’s absolute largest tier-1 megacities (specifically Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Chongqing, and Tianjin).

By centering on dynamic provincial capitals (e.g., Chengdu, Wuhan, Xi’an, Zhengzhou), specialized manufacturing nodes (e.g., Shenzhen, Zhuhai, Taizhou, Zibo), ancient Silk Road oases (e.g., Kashgar, Hotan, Wuwei), and strategic border gateways (e.g., Dandong, Hunchun), this ratlas examines how physical urbanization and infrastructure modernization transformed the full depth of China’s urban hierarchy beyond the global superstar metropolises.

5. Global points of comparison: US benchmark metropolises

How did China’s 30-year urban expansion compare to Western metropolitan development? To establish an objective international baseline, Milwaukee, Chicago, and Charlotte were incorporated into the ratlas using the exact same DLR WSF Evolution 30m satellite data:

Chicago (芝加哥)

Mature Great Lakes Core · Pop: 2.75M (Metro: 9.61M) - 1985 Footprint: 1,145.2 km² (larger than all 47 Chinese cities in 1985) - 2015 Footprint: 1,349.6 km² (+204.4 km²) - 30-Year Multiplier: 1.18× (CAGR: 0.55%) - Comparative Peer: Xi’an / Wuhan
